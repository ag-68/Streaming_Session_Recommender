What are we trying to achieve?

Suppose a viewer liked a sequence of movies, e.g. Star Wars, The Matrix, Fight Club, Pulp Fiction, The Godfather. The home screen still has to suggest a next film that fits that path, not a film with no link to it. The input is one person's liked films, oldest first. The output is a short list of films that are not already in that history. This page compares known ways to make that list.

  • Popularity. Ignores the sequence. Suggests films that many people liked. Every viewer sees the same row. It is the bar the other methods have to clear.
  • Item-to-item. Uses the set of liked films (the sequence above as a bag). Suggests other films liked by people who liked those titles. Order is ignored.
  • Vanilla-SASRec. The only features are the film ids and their order. No genre, no actors, no plot. It scores every remaining film by how well that film fits after the ordered list. The highest scores are the suggestions. The formulas are under Benchmarks & tests.
  • Proposed Enhanced-SASRec. Same backbone as Vanilla, but also uses lower star ratings this viewer already gave (Vanilla only used 4–5★ in the path).
    • Veto-SASRec. A Proposed Enhanced add-on: after the sequence model scores every remaining film, mask out any title this viewer already rated ≤3★ (set those scores to −∞) so they cannot appear in the ranked list. No retrain—only a post-score filter.
    • Polarity-SASRec. A Proposed Enhanced add-on: learned like/dislike polarity embeddings on each history step; trains to predict the next like; can also apply the Veto mask at serve time.

What problem are we solving?

Suppose we line up one viewer's 4- and 5-star films from oldest to newest, hide the last one, and ask Vanilla-SASRec and the Proposed Enhanced variants (plus the simple baselines) to guess it. The method that puts the hidden film nearer the top of its list is the better guess on this test.

  1. 1HistoryLiked films, oldest → newest
  2. 2Hide lastThat film is the answer
  3. 3RankEach method ranks the rest
  4. 4ScoreCloser to top 10 wins

How do raw ratings become sequences?

Suppose the raw file is a long list of star ratings. One person rates Star Wars 5 stars and, later, rates The Matrix, Fight Club, Pulp Fiction, and The Godfather highly. Those rows become steps in that person's liked path. A 2-star rating stays out of the like path, but it still feeds Proposed Enhanced variants (Veto mask / Polarity-SASRec embeddings).

  • Likes → sequence. Ratings of 4–5★, oldest first, form the ordered history Vanilla-SASRec sees.
  • ≤3★ → Veto ban set. Those titles are stored per viewer. After scores are computed, their score slots are set to −∞ so they cannot enter the top-k list (post-score mask; no retrain).
  • ≤2★ → Polarity-SASRec “−” steps. Strong dislikes enter the history with a dislike polarity embedding (likes use a like embedding); the model still predicts the next like.

How do we hide the answer?

Suppose one viewer liked five films. Call them A, B, C, D, and E, from oldest to newest. Training uses A, B, and C. The practice question, which decides when to stop, is: given A, B, and C, name D. The final exam is asked once: given A, B, C, and D, name E. E is never given as an input.

A rating is not proof that the person watched the film to the end. MovieLens only says they gave it stars. If two ratings share a timestamp, the original file order is kept. Film ids are remapped to 1…N. The id 0 is an empty slot, and it is never recommended.

How a ranked list is scored (NDCG@10, HR@10, Coverage@10) is defined under Benchmarks & tests.

What would count as convincing?

Suppose the exam is fair only when every method sees the same history, may not repeat a film already in that history, and is scored by the same rule. The metrics below are that scoring rule. The checks after them are the exam, plus the checks around how the model was trained and how fast it answers.

How is one guess scored?

Each method returns a ranked list over every film the viewer has not already liked. Let r be the 1-based rank of the hidden film. Reported scores are averages over held-out viewers. Higher is better for all three metrics; each lives on a closed scale from 0 to 1.

NDCG@10 (normalized discounted cumulative gain at 10) rewards placing the hidden film nearer the top of the list:

NDCG@10 = 1 / log2(r+1) if r ≤ 10, else 0

If a method puts the hidden film first, that guess scores 1. Position 2 scores 1/log2(3), about 0.63. Position 11 scores 0, because only the top 10 count.

HR@10 (hit rate at 10) asks only whether the hidden film appears somewhere in the top 10. Rank inside that window does not matter:

HR@10 = 1[r ≤ 10]

The reported HR@10 is the share of viewers for whom that indicator is 1. A method that always places the hidden film at position 10 scores the same HR@10 as one that always places it first; NDCG@10 separates those two cases.

Coverage@10 (catalog coverage at 10) measures breadth across viewers, not whether one guess was right:

Coverage@10 = |{films in at least one top-10}| / N

N is the catalog size. A high coverage means the methods’ top-10 lists together touch many distinct films. A low coverage means the lists collapse onto the same few titles. Coverage does not say the hidden film was ranked well.

Paper exam vs our full-catalog exam

SASRec comes from a 2018 paper. That paper counted every star rating as a success, practiced against one random wrong film, and graded the guess among 100 random wrong films (sampled@100). This page’s primary exam keeps ratings of 4 and 5 as likes, and grades the hidden film against every remaining film (full catalog). Sampled scores look much higher (~0.59) than full-catalog (~0.15) for the same model because the exam is easier. Side-by-side numbers for both exams are under Results → Protocol bridge. Paper Table III reports about 0.5905 / 0.8245 on sampled@100; our paper-style run is an approximate recipe in this codebase, not a bit-exact Table III reproduction.

Suppose each full pass through the training films is one epoch. After every epoch we ask the practice question, "given A, B, and C, name D," and we record NDCG@10.

If ten epochs in a row bring no improvement, training stops. The saved model is the epoch with the best practice score. That epoch can be earlier than the last one.

What does each method actually do?

Suppose the methods are given the same history H = (i1, …, iT), oldest first, and the same ban: a film already in H may not be recommended. Index 0 is padding. Catalog ids run from 1 to N. Ties break toward the smaller id.

Popularity

For each film j, count how many training sequences contain j:

cj = #{u : j ∈ Hutrain},   score(j) = cj

The score for every viewer is the same vector c. History is used only to mask films already seen. Empty and unknown histories also use c.

Item-to-item similarity

Build a binary user–film matrix on the training sequences. Entry (u, j) is 1 if viewer u liked film j, else 0. Let vj be column j. Cosine similarity between films a and b is

sim(a, b) = (vaᵀ vb) / (‖va‖₂ ‖vb‖₂)

For each film a, keep the K = 50 films with the largest sim(a, ·), and drop a itself. Given history H, the score of a candidate j is the sum of those stored similarities from every film already in H:

score(j | H) = Σa ∈ H sim(a, j) · 1[j ∈ TopK(a)]

Order inside H is ignored. The sequence Star Wars → … → The Godfather gives the same score vector if the order is shuffled. If H is empty or every id is unknown, the method falls back to popularity.

Vanilla-SASRec

The input is only the ordered film ids. No genre, no actors, no plot. The sequence is left-padded to length L = 100, so empty slots sit on the left and the most recent films sit on the right.

  1. 1Film idsOrdered history, left-padded
  2. 2EmbeddingsItem emb + position emb
  3. 3Blocks2× causal self-attention + FFN
  4. 4hTLast hidden state
  5. 5ScoresDot with item matrix E

Each film j has a learned vector Ej ∈ ℝd with d = 64. Position t has a learned vector Pt ∈ ℝd. Padding uses E0 = 0. The representation at position t starts as

xt = √d · Eit + Pt

That sequence goes through B = 2 blocks. Each block is causal self-attention with one head, then a feed-forward layer, each with a residual connection and layer norm. Causal means position t may use positions 1, …, t only. It may not look at a later film. Dropout is 0.2 during training and 0 when serving.

After the blocks, the last position holds a vector hT ∈ ℝd. The score of every catalog film is the dot product with the same item matrix:

score(j | H) = hTᵀ Ej

Suppose the exact path Star Wars → The Matrix → Fight Club → Pulp Fiction → The Godfather never appears as an adjacent chain in the training file. The model still has a vector for each film. Attention mixes those vectors with their positions. Other training sequences move Ej and the attention weights so that, after many different ordered lists, films that often follow similar lists get a higher score. The method does not look up the title string.

Training asks the same kind of question as the test. At each non-pad position t, the target is the next film it+1. The loss is full-catalog cross-entropy:

ℒ = − Σt : it+1 ≠ 0 log[ exp(htᵀ Eit+1) / Σj=0…N exp(htᵀ Ej) ]

Pad positions with target 0 are ignored. The optimizer is Adam with learning rate 10−3. Each epoch draws a random window of length at most L+1 from each training sequence. After every epoch, validation NDCG@10 is measured. Training stops after 10 epochs with no improvement, and the checkpoint with the best validation NDCG@10 is kept. The seed is 42.

Proposed Enhanced: Veto-SASRec

Veto is a Proposed Enhanced add-on on the sequence scores: after the model scores every catalog film, any title this viewer already rated ≤3★ has its score set to −∞ (masked), so it cannot enter the top-k list. Post-score filter only—no retrain. It can sit on Vanilla scores or on Polarity-SASRec scores.

Proposed Enhanced: Polarity-SASRec

Polarity-SASRec keeps the same causal blocks and full-catalog scoring as Vanilla, but the history is a signed timeline: each step is a film id plus a polarity (like or dislike). Two learned polarity embeddings are added to the item embedding before attention:

xt = √d · Eit + Qpt + Pt

Here pt ∈ {0,1} is dislike vs like, and Q0, Q1 ∈ ℝd are the learned polarity vectors. Strong dislikes (≤2★) enter as pt=0; likes (4–5★) as pt=1. Soft 3★ ratings are not written as signed steps; they still feed the Veto ban set.

  1. 1Signed stepsFilm id + like/dislike
  2. 2EmbeddingsItem + polarity Q + position
  3. 3BlocksSame 2× causal attention
  4. 4hTLast hidden state
  5. 5ScoresDot with E; optional Veto mask

Training supervises next like only (dislike steps are context, not prediction targets). The loss is still full-catalog cross-entropy on those like targets. At serve time the model can apply the same Veto post-score mask so ≤3★ titles stay out of the list.

Side features (optional)

An optional side-feature fusion (genres ⊕ decade ⊕ log-popularity into the item embedding) was measured as a small NDCG lift over id-only Vanilla; it is not the Proposed Enhanced path on this page.
